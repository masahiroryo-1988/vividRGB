Reading this experiment
Count selection and coverage
For K-means, candidate counts are evaluated by held-out silhouette. Full-covariance Gaussian mixtures provide three rules: minimum BIC, smallest K within six BIC units of the minimum (the default), and the original held-out predictive-score rule. All BIC candidates use the identical fixed sample of at most 8,192 locations, three initializations, and the same covariance regularization. Both fit a final model on at most 8,192 locations and assign every valid grid location. Requested component count and occupied group count are both recorded.
BIC = −2 log-likelihood + p log(n), where p = K[d + d(d+1)/2] + K − 1 for full covariance in d dimensions. Smaller is better. Its penalty already discourages complexity; the additional six-unit tolerance favors the smallest near-optimal count. This does not guarantee 4–6 groups. Correlated features and departures from Gaussian mixtures limit interpretation as a true biological group count.
HDBSCAN evaluates nine settings: minimum samples 3, 5 or 10, crossed with minimum cluster sizes of 0.5%, 1% or 2% of grid locations. It fits all grid locations using EOM selection. Settings meeting both targets take priority, followed by the group-count target and coverage. Optional epsilon merging is attempted only if all nine settings exceed ten groups; unsupported attempts are recorded.
What the maps can establish
These are visual feature groups, not individual leaves, organisms, or identified taxa. More coverage does not establish better biological segmentation. Small groups may be meaningful even when a broad-group presentation is preferred.
Whole-image and local 5%, 10%, and 20% DINOv3 features share the original frozen per-image PCA basis. Local crops use 384-pixel enlargement, inverse-aligned four-flip averaging, approximately 75% overlap, and Hann blending. No AnyUp, spatial coordinates, RGB edges or ground-truth masks enter clustering. PCA display channels are independently stretched; clustering uses unwhitened scores. Whole-image processing refers to the existing working image or window; original dimensions and crop coordinates are in the source metadata.


Resampling, feature resolution, and optional completion
Feature grids preserve aspect ratio with a longest side of 256. Interpolation and overlapping crops create correlated samples; these are not independent pixel observations. Eight-by-eight spatial blocks are shuffled into three folds. Each fold uses up to 3,072 training locations and 1,024 held-out locations, identical across candidate counts. Shared DINO context across block borders remains, so this is a within-image robustness check, not independent biological validation.
Under the predictive-score rule, the count is closest to five among converged candidates within one standard error of the best mean score. BIC rules instead use the minimum BIC and an optional six-unit tolerance. Stronger evidence may select counts outside 4–6. The final full-grid occupied count can differ from the number of fitted Gaussian components. Stability is pairwise adjusted Rand agreement of the selected count across the three fits on a fixed 2,048-location probe set.
Optional HDBSCAN completion uses 15 neighbours in feature space, drawn from assigned members with HDBSCAN strength at least 0.5. An unassigned point is filled only when inverse-distance voting agrees at least 80% and mean neighbour distance is within twice the cluster-specific 95th percentile reference distance. These are explicit heuristic gates, not calibrated probabilities. Completion does not force 85% coverage. Gray shows remaining unassigned locations; the raw map remains available. A cluster can occupy disconnected regions.
The ten examples per domain were frozen from the existing collection. They are illustrative examples, not a representative random population sample. Histograms from different feature approaches reuse the same images and should not be pooled as independent observations.
